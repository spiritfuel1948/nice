## 1. Premise & Theme (Lesson 1 + [Step 3 Workshop](../05-premise-workshop.md))

**Premise (chosen, option C, series-wide):**
> When the peace between knights and wizards depends on two teenage hostages,
> a squire and a wizard who hate each other must reach the summit alive, or
> else the war will restart. But an assassin's attack has tied their lives into
> a single thread that every spell wears thinner, and each of them is hiding
> something that could break the peace for good.

**Tagline for covers and ads:** *She was sent to kill him. Now if he dies, so does she.*

**Theme question:** *Is doing what you're told the same as doing what's right?*

**Audience for the first series:** ☐ Kids (6–9) ☑ **Teens (YA)** ☐ Adults

**Tone:** ☐ Cozy ☑ **Classic adventure** ☐ Dark/gritty ☐ Romantasy ☐ Other: ______

**Core relationship:** ☑ **A knight and a wizard forced together**

**The chain (why they can't walk away):** **Treaty Wards + Heart-Thread**
(picked for marketability; can still change). The knight and the wizard are
enemy peace-hostages sent to a summit together. An assassin attacks them, and
a desperate spell saves them by linking their lives: if one dies, both die.
See [brainstorm-the-chain.md](brainstorm-the-chain.md#the-pick-treaty-wards--heart-thread).

**Heart-Thread rules:**
- If one dies, both die.
- **Every spell the wizard casts frays the thread**, shortening the time they
  have left. So the wizard has to rely on the knight's sword.
- **How it can be undone:** only with **the knights' greatest relic**, the same
  one the tower ordered Emrys to steal. *Steal it* and they're free, but the
  treaty breaks and the war returns. *Leave it* and they stay bound while every
  spell burns their shared life. That one object is the series' **prize**
  (road, team, prize).

**The relic: The Last Dragon's Heart.** The heart of the last dragon, still
beating in the knights' chapel after three hundred years.
- **Cure:** its fire unmakes any spell, including the Heart-Thread.
- **Why the tower wants it** *(fits Life-Spark)*: a dragon's life is vast. A
  wizard who draws on the heart could cast without burning their own life, which
  means unlimited power. That's a clear motive for the adults who sent Emrys.
- **Moral question:** is it still alive? Is it suffering? Could it wake?
  *(A possible hook for Season 2.)*
- **Idea to confirm in Step 5:** the first oath-steel was forged in dragon fire,
  which would explain why it resists magic.
- Not used: shared emotions, shared injuries, distance sickness. They can be
  apart, but the cost of magic keeps them needing each other.

**Romance:** A **hint of slow-burn**: sparks and tension, nothing official in
Part 1. It stays a classic adventure.

**Point of view:** **Alternating chapters**, knight and wizard, in third person
limited (Lesson 11).

**Hook (for the blurb):** *She was sent to kill him. Now if he dies, so does she.*

**The mirror:** both were sent with secret orders. She's ordered to kill him if
the peace fails, and he's ordered to make sure it fails. Both are tools of the
adults who sent them, so the theme is tested on **both** heroes. **Planned
reveal (Part 3, the season's All Is Lost):** each finds out the other's orders
on the same night.

**Theme question (draft):** *Is doing what you're told the same as doing what's right?*

*Why these picks (marketability):* the girl-knight/charming-wizard pairing
matches the comps (*Sorcery of Thorns*, *Alanna*). Grumpy vs. sunshine gives
banter readers quote. "Sent to kill the person you're bound to" is the same
trope family publishers are selling now. And each Lie is tested by the plot in
every chapter (Weiland). Names, genders, and details can all change.

## 3. Magic System (Lesson 3)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Source:** where does the power come from? | **Life-Spark.** Wizards burn their *own life* to cast. |
| **Access:** who can use it, and how do they learn? | |
| **Cost:** what does each spell take? | Hours of life for small spells, **years** for great ones. Wizards look young but die early. Since the Heart-Thread, Emrys and Maren share **one** life, so every spell he casts spends her time too. |
| **Limits:** what can it **never** do? | **It can't break oath-steel.** |
| **Weakness / counter:** what stops it? | **Oath-forged steel:** a blade forged while a knight speaks their oath can cut a spell in half, and armor forged the same way turns magic aside. That's why knights were the only thing wizards feared in the old war, and why Maren's sword can do what Emrys's magic can't. |
| **Society:** how is it treated (respected, licensed, feared, hunted)? | **The tower owns its wizards through the Life-Ledger.** The tower trains you, so it owns your life. Every spell you cast in its service is recorded in the great Ledger. Only when the debt is paid do you earn a **Master's star**, and your remaining years become your own. *That's what Emrys wants.* **The tower's offer to him:** steal the Dragon's Heart, and his debt is wiped clean. **Ideas to add emotional weight:** (1) the tower made Emrys cast big spells as a child prodigy, so he has **fewer years left than he admits**; (2) the Ledger can't tell whose life he's spending since the Heart-Thread, so it may be recording *Maren's* years as tower debt. |
| **Signature:** what does casting look, sound, and feel like? | |
| **Hard or soft?** What does the reader learn, and when? | |

**The rules the climax depends on:**
1. **Oath-steel cuts spells**, but only while the oath is kept.
2. **A broken oath kills the blade** until a truer oath is sworn.
3. **Every spell burns life**, and since the Heart-Thread, their *shared* life.

**Climax shape:** Maren's reawakened blade, sworn on her own oath, plus *one
last spell* from Emrys, which costs them both, does what neither could alone.
**Show rules 1 and 2 early in Part 1** (for example, a disgraced knight's dead
blade) so the payoff feels fair (Sanderson's First Law).

## 5. Lore & History (Lesson 5)

**The war's origin: nobody agrees why it started.** Knights say the wizards
started it, wizards say the knights did, and peasants tell a third story. The
truth is buried (Lesson 5: lore that's partly wrong). Maren and Emrys each grew
up believing one version, so **their arguments are the war in miniature.** The
truth is revealed in **Part 4**.
- **What really happened (the author knows; readers find out in Part 4):**
  **One false accusation.** Three hundred years ago, a wizard prince was found
  dead with a knight's dagger in him. The knight was innocent, framed, but
  nobody asked questions. Orders were given, and three centuries of war
  followed. *That's the theme in history: obeying without asking "is this
  right?"* **The deeper mystery: who framed the knight, and why?**
- *Draft versions (change freely):*
  - **Wizards' version:** "A knight murdered our prince in cold blood. We only
    defended ourselves." (They believe the frame.)
  - **Knights' version:** "The wizards executed an innocent knight without
    trial. They wanted the Dragon's Heart and used a lie as their excuse."
  - **Peasants' version (closest to the truth):** "The prince and the knight
    were *friends*, and somebody couldn't stand a knight and a wizard being
    close." *(A quiet echo of Maren and Emrys.)*
- *Idea:* the assassin's attack on Maren and Emrys could be **staged to frame
  one side again**. History repeating itself is a strong clue for readers.
