# Publishing & Money: Amazon KDP, Step by Step

**KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing)** is Amazon's free self-publishing platform for
ebooks, paperbacks, and hardcovers. It's the easiest place to start. This guide
walks through it in order and covers the other stores at the end.

> **Rules change.** Everything here was checked in **October 2026**. Before each
> launch, re-check the linked KDP help pages. Amazon changed royalties in 2025
> and 2026.

## Step 1 — Set up your KDP account
1. Sign up at kdp.amazon.com. It's free.
2. Enter your **legal name** for identity verification, plus your **tax
   information** and **bank account** for royalty payments.
3. **Pen name (optional):** Your legal name stays private, and the author name
   on the book can be a pen name. It **can't be changed after publishing**, and
   it should stay consistent across a series.
   ([KDP: Authors & Contributors](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G2BWJN2BY98T5PV2))

## Step 2 — Finish the manuscript
Follow the [Craft Guide](01-craft-guide.md), then revise in layers (Lesson 13).

## Step 3 — Editing
Median **fiction** editing rates from the Editorial Freelancers Association's
2026 survey, as reported by
[EditFast](https://editfast.com/faq/editing-costs/) and
[ebookpbook](https://www.ebookpbook.com/2026/04/29/book-editing-cost-types/).
The EFA doesn't set rates; these are surveyed medians.

| Edit type | What it fixes | Rate | For a 25,000-word novella |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Developmental** | Big picture: plot, arc, pacing | 3.0–3.5¢/word | ~$750–875 |
| **Line edit** | Sentence style and flow | 2.7–3.5¢/word | ~$675–875 |
| **Copyedit** | Grammar, consistency, clarity | 2.0–2.7¢/word | ~$500–675 |
| **Proofread** | Final typos | 1.2–2.0¢/word | ~$300–500 |

**On a tight budget:** use beta readers plus your own careful self-edit, then
hire a proofreader at minimum. (I can also give you detailed chapter critiques
for free.)

## Step 4 — Formatting
- **Kindle Create** is Amazon's free tool. It handles normal text books
  ("reflowable") and picture books ("fixed layout").
  ([Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/kdp-children-book-formatting/))
- KDP also accepts a Word (.docx) file directly.
- Paid tools like Vellum (Mac only) and Atticus make prettier interiors, but
  they don't export fixed-layout picture books.

## Step 5 — The cover (your #1 sales tool)
Readers judge books by their covers, at thumbnail size. A fantasy cover has to
**look like the other bestsellers in your subgenre**, so readers know instantly
what kind of book it is.

| Option | Typical cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **DIY** (e.g., Canva) | $0–50 | Risky for fantasy, which usually needs illustration |
| **Premade cover** | ~$69–150 | Already made to genre standards; you get your title and name added. Best value for a first book. |
| **Custom cover** | ~$880 average; fantasy median ~$910 | Reedsy's data from 9,600+ cover projects in 2025 |

([Reedsy cover cost](https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost),
[Draft2Digital](https://draft2digital.com/blog/?p=755))

- **Ebooks** need a front cover only. **Paperbacks** need a full wrap: front,
  spine, and back.
- **Series:** use one consistent design (same fonts and layout, new image and
  color per part) so the books look like a set.
- If the cover art is AI-generated, it **must be disclosed** (see Step 9).

## Step 6 — Title, series info, and description
- **Series name and number:** Enter only the series name (e.g., *The Oathbound
  Chronicles*) and the number as a digit ("1", not "Book 1"). KDP builds a
  **series page** automatically, usually within 72 hours.
  ([KDP: Start a Book Series](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GMFKBUS43QQ5AJ5A))
- **Book description (the "blurb"):** this is a sales pitch, not a summary.
  A simple structure:
  1. **Hook line:** the most intriguing thing about the story
  2. **Hero + situation:** who they are and what disrupts their life
  3. **Stakes + choice:** what they must do and what they'll lose if they fail
  4. **A closing question or tagline**
  5. **One line of "comps" and length:** e.g., *"A 25,000-word novella for fans
     of [similar books]. Part 1 of the series."* Telling readers the length up
     front prevents "too short!" reviews.

## Step 7 — Categories and keywords
- You get **3 categories** and **7 keywords**.
  ([KDP Keywords](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201298500),
  [KDP Categories](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200652170))
- Good keywords describe the **setting, character type, plot, and tone**. For
  example: *"knight and wizard," "sword and sorcery for teens," "found family
  fantasy," "magic academy."* Short phrases readers actually type into search.
- **Don't** repeat your title or categories, claim quality ("best"), use
  time-sensitive words ("new," "on sale"), or use brand names you don't own.
- You can change keywords and categories anytime after publishing.

## Step 8 — Reading age (kids' and teen books)
Set the **Reading Age**: 6–8 for early chapter books, 9–12 for middle grade,
13–17 for YA. Without it, a kids' or teen book may not appear in those
categories or in age-filtered search.
([KDP: Primary Audience & Reading Age](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201506310))

## Step 9 — The AI question
When you publish, KDP asks whether the book contains AI-generated content.
([KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390))
- **AI-generated means you must disclose.** Text, images, or translations an AI
  *created*, even if you edited them heavily afterward. That includes cover and
  interior art.
- **AI-assisted means no disclosure needed.** You wrote it yourself and used AI
  to brainstorm, edit, error-check, or improve it.
- Either way, **you're responsible** for making sure nothing infringes
  copyright.
- **Copyright:** U.S. law requires a **human author**. In *Thaler v.
  Perlmutter* (D.C. Circuit, March 2025), a federal appeals court confirmed that
  work generated entirely by AI isn't copyrightable.
  ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/thaler-v-perlmutter))
  **Writing the story yourself protects your ownership.**

## Step 10 — Ebook pricing and royalties
([KDP eBook Royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200644210),
[List Price Requirements](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200634560))

| Royalty option | Price (Amazon.com) | What you earn |
|---|---|---|
| **70%** | **$2.99–$12.99** (since July 7, 2026) | 70% of the price, minus a delivery fee of **$0.15 per MB** |
| **35%** | below $2.99 or above $12.99 | 35% of the price, no delivery fee |

**Rules worth knowing:**
- To get 70%, the ebook must be priced **at least 20% below** your paperback.
- Books that are mostly **public-domain** content only get 35%. Original stories
  *inspired* by Arthurian legend are fine; reprinting the old texts is not.
- During a **Kindle Countdown Deal** (Step 11), you keep 70% even below $2.99.
- A text-only novella file is tiny (well under 1 MB), so the delivery fee is
  only a few cents.

**What short fiction usually costs** (author surveys and practice,
[Lindsay Buroker](https://lindsayburoker.com/e-publishing/my-answers-to-ebook-pricing-questions/)):
short stories often sell for $0.99. Works of 15,000–45,000 words are commonly
$2.99. In one survey of romance novellas, most were priced $2.99–$3.99.

**Suggested price plan for a novella series:**

| Product | Price | You earn (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 (launch week / promo) | $0.99 | ~$0.35 |
| Part 1 (regular) and Parts 2–4 | $2.99–$3.99 | ~$2.03–$2.73 |
| Season omnibus (4 parts) ebook | $7.99–$9.99 | ~$5.44–$6.84 |

## Step 11 — KDP Select (Kindle Unlimited) or selling "wide"?
This is the biggest business decision you'll make.

**KDP Select** ([KDP Select](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200798990),
[Free Book Promotions](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201298240)):
- Your **ebook** is exclusive to Amazon for **90 days**, renewing automatically
  unless you opt out. **Paperbacks and audiobooks aren't restricted.**
- Your book joins **Kindle Unlimited (KU)**, Amazon's subscription. You're paid
  per **page read** (KENP) from a monthly fund. Third-party trackers estimate
  about **$0.0048 per page** in 2026; it changes monthly.
  ([Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/kdp-select-or-not-is-kindle-unlimited-worth-it/),
  [Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/ku-vs-wide-what-the-data-actually-says/))
- Each 90-day term you get **one** promotion: **up to 5 free days** *or* a
  **Kindle Countdown Deal**.

**Selling "wide"** (everywhere):
- **Draft2Digital** delivers your ebook to Apple Books, Kobo, Barnes & Noble,
  libraries, and more. It keeps about 10%, on top of each store's own cut.
- **IngramSpark** puts *print* books into bookstores and libraries.
- Wide lets you make Part 1 **permanently free**, which is a strong series
  hook. That isn't allowed inside KDP Select.
  ([ScribeCount on permafree](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/publishing-on-amazon/permafree-books-on-amazon),
  [Draft2Digital vs. KDP](https://www.publishing.co.uk/guides/draft2digital-vs-kdp/),
  [Author Media](https://www.authormedia.com/should-you-leave-kindle-unlimited-in-2026/))

| | KDP Select (KU) | Wide |
|---|---|---|
| Simplicity | ✅ One platform | More accounts to manage |
| Series readers | ✅ KU readers try new authors for "free" | Builds slowly across stores |
| Free promotions | 5 free days per 90 days | ✅ Permanently free Part 1 possible |
| Risk | All your ebook income depends on Amazon | ✅ Spread out |

**My recommendation for you:** Start in **KDP Select** for the first 90 days. It's
the simplest setup, and KU readers often binge series. Review your page-read
income at the end of the term and decide whether to stay or go wide.

## Step 12 — The paperback
- **ISBN:** KDP gives you one **free**. The listed publisher becomes
  "Independently published," and that ISBN can only be used on KDP. Your own
  ISBN from Bowker costs **$125 for one or $295 for ten**. Ebooks don't need an
  ISBN.
  ([Vappingo on ISBNs](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/?p=11958))
- **Royalty formula:** (royalty rate × list price) − printing cost.
  - **60%** on Amazon.com if priced **$9.99 or more**; **50%** if priced below
    $9.99 (since June 2025)
  - **Expanded Distribution** (other stores): **40%**
  - **KDP's own example:** a 333-page black-and-white paperback priced at $15
    costs about $5.00 to print. 0.60 × $15 − $5.00 = **$4.00**.
    ([KDP Paperback Royalty](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330))
- **What this means for a short series:** A single 100-page novella paperback
  earns very little per copy. **Combine the parts into an omnibus paperback**
  (around 300+ pages, priced $14.99–$16.99) so it lands in the 60% tier with a
  healthy margin.
- KDP **can't** make paperback box sets, so the omnibus is published as its own
  book.

## Step 13 — Launch and marketing (simple version)
1. **Back matter.** At the end of every book: a link to the next part, plus an
   invitation to your **newsletter**.
2. **Reader magnet.** Offer a free **prequel short story** or bonus chapter for
   joining your newsletter. A 10,000–20,000-word prequel novella is called "the
   gold standard."
   ([Manuscript Report](https://manuscriptreport.com/blog/reader-magnets-for-authors))
3. **Advance readers.** Give early copies to readers who agree to post honest
   reviews at launch. **Never pay for reviews or trade them.** Amazon prohibits
   that.
4. **Launch-week price.** $0.99 for Part 1, or use the KDP Select free days.
5. **Read-through is the business model.** Your money comes from readers who
   buy Parts 2, 3, and 4. Free or cheap first books pay off best with **4–6+
   books** in the series.
   ([ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/business-strategies/catalog-wide-pricing-architecture-for-indie-authors))
6. **Social media.** BookTok is repeatedly named as a driver of YA and
   romantasy discovery. Short videos about your world, characters, or "tropes
   in this book" work well.
7. **Paid ads (Amazon Ads)** come *later*. *(My advice.)* Ads work best once
   you have 2–3 books, so a click on Part 1 can lead to more sales.

## Step 14 — Protect your work
- In the U.S., copyright exists automatically when you write something
  original. **Registering** with the U.S. Copyright Office (copyright.gov, for a
  small fee) is optional, but you'd need it to sue an infringer in the U.S.
- Keep dated copies of your drafts (this git repository works for that).
- **Taxes:** royalties are income. Keep receipts for covers, editing, and ads.
  Ask a tax professional about your situation.

## Budget tiers for your first book

| Tier | Total | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lean** | $0–200 | Premade or DIY cover, self-edit plus beta readers, free KDP ISBN, Kindle Create |
| **Moderate** | $500–1,500 | Premade cover (~$100) + proofread (~$400) + copyedit (~$600) |
| **Professional** | $2,000–5,000 | Custom illustrated cover (~$900) + developmental edit (~$800) + copyedit + proofread |
| **Picture book** (if you go that way) | $2,500–7,000+ | Above + illustrations ($2,000–6,000) + fixed-layout formatting |

**Strategy:** go lean or moderate on Part 1, then put sales income back into
later parts. These costs repeat for **every part** in a series, so short parts
need lean production.

## Pre-launch checklist
- [ ] Manuscript revised, beta-read, and proofread
- [ ] Cover (ebook front; full wrap if paperback)
- [ ] Title, subtitle, **series name and number**
- [ ] Blurb (hook → hero → stakes → question → length/comps line)
- [ ] 3 categories and 7 keywords chosen
- [ ] Reading age set (kids'/teen books)
- [ ] AI disclosure answered honestly
- [ ] Price set (70% band: $2.99–$12.99; at least 20% below the paperback)
- [ ] KDP Select: yes or no
- [ ] Back matter: next-book link + newsletter link
- [ ] Advance readers lined up
- [ ] Next part already being written
