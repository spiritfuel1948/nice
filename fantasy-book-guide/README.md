## The 5 biggest things the research taught me

1. **Readers want limits, not raw power, from magic.** A wizard who can do
   anything is boring. A wizard whose spell costs them something they love is
   a story. ([Sanderson's Laws](01-craft-guide.md#lesson-3--magic-systems-your-wizards))
2. **A character arc works like this:** the hero believes a **Lie** and wants the
   wrong thing. The plot forces them to discover a **Truth** and what they actually
   need. That change is what readers remember.
   ([Weiland](01-craft-guide.md#lesson-7--character-arcs-how-people-change))
3. **Short, multi-part stories work when each part closes one story thread and
   leaves a bigger one open.** This is the MICE Quotient. Each part feels
   satisfying, and readers still buy the next one.
   ([Lesson 10](01-craft-guide.md#lesson-10--short-stories-and-multi-part-series))
4. **"Same story for kids, teens, and adults" really means one world with three
   books.** Each age group needs a different-aged hero, different length, and
   different content limits. Share the world, not the manuscript.
   ([Age plan](02-age-editions-and-series-plan.md))
5. **Amazon changed the money in 2026.** Ebooks priced **$2.99–$12.99** now earn
   **70%** (the cap was $9.99 until July 7, 2026). New books are capped at **2 per
   format per week** (since Sept 21, 2026). AI-generated text or art must be
   **disclosed**. ([Publishing guide](03-publishing-and-money.md))

## How we write: AI drafts, you review and edit

**Your decision (Oct 20, 2026):** AI writes the drafts, and you review and
edit them before they go into the book. That's allowed on Amazon. Here's what
comes with it:

- **You must tick "AI-generated" when you publish on KDP.** Text an AI created
  counts as AI-generated *even after heavy edits*.
  ([KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390))
  Amazon has been enforcing this rule.
- **Copyright covers your human contribution, not the raw AI text.** The U.S.
  Copyright Office (January 2025) said prompts alone aren't enough for
  authorship. Your own creative edits and additions *are* protected.
  ([IPWatchdog summary](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/))
  **The more you rewrite, the more of the book you own.**
- **Quality is on the review.** Readers notice flat, generic AI prose, and
  reviews drive sales. The review checklist in
  [`my-series/ai-use-log.md`](my-series/ai-use-log.md) is what keeps the book
  sounding human.

**My role:** I draft scenes from your story bible, and you review every piece
with the checklist, change what doesn't sound right, and log your edits. I
still teach, brainstorm, critique, and handle blurbs, keywords, and publishing.
You can switch back to writing yourself at any time. Your choices and plot
decisions stay yours either way.
