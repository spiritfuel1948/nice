# Step 3 Workshop: Your Knight & Wizard, Forced Together

**Your choices so far:** ☑ Teens (YA) ☑ Classic adventure ☑ A knight and a
wizard forced together

This workshop turns those three choices into your **premise** and **theme**.
You'll write them; I'll critique them and push them further.

---

## 1. What your choices promise readers

Each choice is a promise. Readers who pick up your book will expect these things.

**YA (teens, plus the many adults who read YA)**
- Heroes aged **16–17**. A 13-year-old reads as middle grade, and an 18+
  college-age hero pushes the book toward "new adult."
  ([Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/the-key-differences-between-middle-grade-vs-young-adult))
- Teen-sized themes: **identity, independence, first real loyalty, finding out
  the adults were wrong.**
- "PG-13" content: real danger and battles with real consequences. A crush or
  slow-burn is fine, but in a classic adventure the romance isn't the center.
  Mild language.

**Classic adventure**
- A **quest or journey**, a **clear threat**, **wonder**, **battles**, and
  **friendship**. Good wins in the end, but it costs something.
- A hopeful ending, even if it's bittersweet.

**Knight + wizard forced together**
- This combines two of *Save the Cat!*'s story "genres"
  ([Save the Cat](https://savethecat.com/?p=11992),
  [SceneOne](https://SceneOne.app/blog/the-ten-save-the-cat-story-genres/)):
  - **Buddy Love:** an **incomplete hero** who's missing something, a
    **counterpart** who has it, and a **complication** that threatens the
    relationship. Both leads carry roughly equal weight, and the big question
    is *"Will this partnership survive?"*
  - **Golden Fleece**, the quest story: a **road**, a **team**, and a
    **prize**. The prize must be clear, specific, and seem almost impossible
    to reach.
- So your story needs **both** a relationship question ("Will they become a
  real team?") **and** a quest question ("Will they get the prize?").

## 3. The 5 ingredients of a great forced pairing

### Ingredient 1: The Chain (why they can't just walk away)
The chain must be strong enough that splitting up means disaster. The best
chains connect to your **magic system**, because limits create drama
(Sanderson's Second Law). Pick one of these or invent your own:

| Chain | Example shape |
|---|---|
| **Magical bond** | A spell links them. If one dies, both die, or they can't go more than a mile apart. |
| **Orders** | The king or the knightly order commands it, and refusing means exile or execution. |
| **Shared blame** | Both are accused of the same crime and must clear their names together. |
| **Escort mission** | One must deliver the other somewhere, alive. |
| **The prize needs both** | The door, relic, or curse can only be opened or broken by a sword *and* a spell together. |
| **A debt or oath** | The knight owes the wizard a life-debt and is sworn to serve until it's repaid. |
| **Last survivors** | Everyone else is gone, and neither can make it alone. |

**Pro tip:** Start with an **outside chain** (orders, a spell, a mission).
Later, when the chain breaks or could be broken, have them **choose** to stay
together. That choice is one of the strongest emotional beats in the story.

### Ingredient 2: Opposite values (where your theme comes from)
Each of them holds **half the truth**. Pick a pair of opposite values, or
invent one:

| Knight might stand for… | Wizard might stand for… |
|---|---|
| rules | freedom |
| duty | curiosity |
| strength | knowledge |
| faith | doubt |
| tradition | change |
| honor | survival |

Your **theme question** comes out of that conflict. For example: *"When should
you break a rule to do what's right?"* or *"Is knowing the truth worth losing
what you believe in?"*

### Ingredient 3: Each has what the other lacks
This is the "incomplete hero" from Buddy Love, at three levels:
- **Skills:** sword vs. spell, endurance vs. cleverness
- **Personality:** brave but rigid vs. clever but reckless
- **Emotional:** one can't trust anyone, and the other trusts too easily

### Ingredient 4: Mirror Lies
Their Lies (Lesson 6) should **clash**, so each one is the other's teacher.
*Pattern example (make your own):* the knight believes *"If I follow every
rule, I'll be worthy,"* and the wizard believes *"I can only rely on myself."*
Each one's Truth is learned partly from the other.

### Ingredient 5: The complication (the outside force that pulls them apart)
A villain, a secret, or a choice that **tests the bond**. For example: one of
them was secretly ordered to betray the other; or saving the partner means
failing the quest; or the order or the wizards' guild demands they turn on
each other.

## 4. The relationship arc, mapped onto the beats

| Beat (Lesson 9) | What happens between them |
|---|---|
| **Opening** (0–10%) | Each one alone, showing their Lie and what they lack |
| **Catalyst** (~10%) | **The chain locks.** They're stuck together. |
| **Debate / Break into Two** (10–20%) | Open hostility. They try to escape the chain and fail. |
| **Fun & Games** (20–50%) | Bickering on the road. Their first win only happens *because* they combined skills. Grudging respect. |
| **Midpoint** (~50%) | Real trust: they save each other, **or** a secret comes out |
| **Bad Guys Close In** (50–75%) | The outside threat grows *and* the secret or suspicion eats at them |
| **All Is Lost** (~75%) | **The bond breaks:** a betrayal, a separation, or the mission chosen over each other |
| **Dark Night** (75–80%) | Each one alone again realizes what the other taught them (the Truth) |
| **Finale** (80–99%) | They come back **by choice** and combine sword and spell in a way neither could alone, using a magic rule the reader learned earlier (Sanderson's First Law) |
| **Final Image** | A mirror of the opening: two loners are now a team |

**For a 4-part season**, the relationship arc can stretch across the books
while each part still has its own complete mini-quest:
- **Part 1:** Forced together, ending in grudging respect. The first mini-quest
  is solved.
- **Part 2:** Real trust builds, then a secret is revealed.
- **Part 3:** The bond breaks. This is the season's All Is Lost.
- **Part 4:** They choose each other and win the season's prize.
